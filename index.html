Mayo High School · Student-Founded, Student-Run

One community.
Three equal pillars.

Mayo ASA is building an organized Asian American, Pacific Islander, and Desi student community — and we're not stopping at culture. Community, education, and advocacy carry equal weight here.

Join Mayo ASA Read our mission

Not a hierarchy. A braid.

Most cultural clubs stop at potlucks and performances. Mayo ASA treats community, education, and advocacy as three strands of the same rope — each one holding up the other two. That third strand, advocacy, is what makes this more than a cultural club.

We build a real network of students and allies, teach the history that classrooms often skip, and turn what we learn into concrete change at Mayo.

What we're building

Three pillars, one club

Education

Advocacy

Change something real

Identify problems, gather evidence, work with administrators, team up with other student organizations, and push for concrete change.

See the plan →

We are not "anti" anyone. Mayo ASA exists to build something, not to compete with anyone else building something good. The Black Student Union isn't our competitor — organizations like BSU, the Equity Team, and Global Affairs are exactly the kind of groups we want standing next to us.

Different communities carry different histories and face stereotyping in different ways. We'd rather understand that together than pretend one club can speak for every experience. See how we're approaching coalition work.

Who started this

Founders

Mayo ASA was founded by two Mayo students who wanted more than a cultural club on campus.

WW
Wilson Wang Co-Founder

Co-founded Mayo ASA to give Mayo's Asian, Asian American, Pacific Islander, and Desi students a community that also does the work of advocacy, not just culture.

CL
Chen Liang Co-Founder

Co-founded Mayo ASA alongside Wilson, focused on making sure the club's education and advocacy work is grounded and concrete from day one.
